# Content, editorial quality and trust

## Begin with a useful answer
For each important page state the intended reader, task and next useful action. Identify what the page contributes that a generic summary does not: first-hand observations, original data, a working tool, clear instructions, verifiable product details, useful comparisons or expert interpretation. Do not invent experience, qualifications, interviews, results or citations to satisfy a trust checklist. [G03]

Evaluate whether the reader can complete the task without wading through repetitive introductions, generic conclusions, filler FAQs or unexplained jargon. Preserve nuance and limitations. Match detail to the task rather than a fixed word count or competitors’ average length. An answer can be brief when that is enough. [G01]

## Claims and accountability
Maintain a claim-to-source list for factual assertions that matter. Identify the author/reviewer only when those people actually contributed and approved their role. For high-stakes topics, require appropriate professional review and current primary sources. About, contact, ownership, correction and policy information should reflect the real organisation; use what is appropriate to the site, not an invented universal page-count requirement. [G03]

Distinguish a substantive update from a date change. Do not change publication dates, “tested” labels, prices, testimonials or review counts merely to simulate freshness. AI assistance is not a substitute for accountable fact checking. Avoid large volumes of derivative pages produced primarily to manipulate discovery. [G07, G02]

## Titles, descriptions and headings
Write a distinctive title that accurately identifies the page’s subject and useful distinction. Avoid shared defaults, misleading promises, repetitive boilerplate and long lists of variants. Search engines may construct a displayed title differently. Treat length previews as display aids, not universal character-limit ranking rules. [G14]

A description can be a useful concise summary and reason to choose the page; it is not a guaranteed displayed snippet. Write natural, accurate descriptions for important pages and use safe meaningful generation for appropriate template families. Do not claim direct ranking gains from filling a description field. [G15]

Use headings to organise the answer and support navigation. A clear primary heading is usually good editorial structure, but multiple H1 elements do not establish a ranking violation. Check readability, hierarchy and meaning rather than counting tags as a proxy for quality. [G01]

## Natural language and questions
Use the audience’s terminology and relevant entities where they help understanding. Variants should follow the subject naturally; do not insert cities, celebrities, competitors or famous brands unrelated to the offering. A factually justified comparison may name products accurately without manufactured endorsement. Keywords are research evidence, not a quota.

Add FAQs only for genuine questions not already answered well in context. Choose a helpful location: sometimes the answer belongs beside the relevant feature or price rather than in a separate FAQ block. Label unverified candidate questions as hypotheses. FAQ content and Google’s former FAQ rich-result feature are different things; read the current structured-data guidance in this pack.

## Content review outcomes
Choose retain, improve, consolidate, correct, retire or create. Explain the rationale and protect useful historical information, conversions and legitimate long-tail needs. A traffic decline or low word count alone is insufficient reason to delete a page. Validate risky changes with the business/content owner and retain rollback.

Sources: G01–G03, G07, G14, G15. These review questions are an operational synthesis, not a content scoring formula.
