# Evidence-led first-pass keyword research

## What this workflow can and cannot establish
With permitted web search, an agent can discover vocabulary, inspect result composition, identify user questions and develop a defensible first-pass intent/content plan. It cannot infer reliable absolute demand, difficulty or conversions from a handful of results. Without browsing, deliver **unvalidated hypotheses** plus a validation plan, not “completed keyword research”.

Start with the offering, constraints, audience, buying/learning tasks, markets, languages, business goals and existing URL inventory. Exclude subjects the organisation cannot credibly serve. A popular query outside the offering is not automatically an opportunity.

## 1. Collect and classify evidence
Use available first-party search queries, landing-page performance, site search, support questions and sales language. Remove identifying/customer information. Request authorised exports rather than inventing access. Search Console measures the property’s observed search exposure; it is not a market-volume tool. [G40]

Expand with permitted web search, relevant primary references, legitimate result questions/autocomplete when actually visible, and licensed keyword data when provided. Record whether each row came from observation, a vendor, a user, or an LLM idea. Do not claim to have seen “People also ask” through a search API that did not expose it.

Provider metrics need provider, definition, country, language, date range, collection date and available units. Google Trends values are normalised relative interest, not absolute monthly searches. Ads planning outputs are estimates in their documented scope; advertising competition is not organic ranking difficulty. Different vendors’ keyword difficulty scales are not interchangeable. [G42, G43, I01, I03]

## 2. Inspect representative search results
For each material cluster, record query, actual search tool/engine, language/market, date, device when controllable, observed result types and a few genuinely relevant URLs. Distinguish organic pages, ads, maps, products, video and generated answers. Inspect destination pages to understand task and substance, not to copy their wording or outline.

A generic search tool may mix locations, cache results or use an undisclosed provider. State these limitations. Do not label its output a reproducible local Google rank check. Do not bypass CAPTCHAs or vendor restrictions to obtain a cleaner-looking dataset.

Read what users appear to need: a direct answer, category/listing, product, comparison, tutorial, local provider, tool, troubleshooting or navigation to a known brand. Separate observed result patterns from your interpretation. Results suggest intent; they do not prove a single intent or prescribe identical content. [I02]

## 3. Build head and long-tail opportunities
Use broader topic terms to understand the landscape and specific needs to find useful subtopics. “Long-tail” refers to the demand distribution, not a minimum number of words. A short obscure query can be long-tail; a long common phrase need not be. Without volume evidence, label a candidate **specific query; tail classification unverified**. [I04]

Expand by legitimate audience, problem, constraints, features, compatibility, alternatives, comparison, process stage, error, location and timing. Only use modifiers that fit the real offering. Generate no fake local presence or impossible compatibility claims. Model-generated variants are ideas until corroborated.

## 4. Cluster by task, then map URLs
Use shared user intent, content purpose and observed result overlap as evidence. Do not impose a universal percentage-overlap threshold. Keep supporting synonyms and close variants on a strong existing page where one answer serves them. Create a separate page only when a materially distinct task, format, audience, local service or product merits its own useful destination. [I01, I02, I04]

For each cluster choose **existing page retained**, **existing page improved**, **distinct new page proposed**, **consolidation investigation**, **rejected**, or **deferred**. Multiple ranking URLs can be legitimate; investigate actual intent overlap and outcomes before calling it harmful cannibalisation. [I05]

## 5. Prioritise transparently
Assess business fit, evidence of need, current coverage, ability to add unique value, result-page fit, realistic competition, effort and dependencies. Use high/medium/low with a written explanation rather than a fabricated precision score. Do not equate a vendor’s authority score with an engine metric or guarantee easier rankings from a low difficulty number.

Address broken or inadequate existing pages before proposing hundreds of new URLs. A useful result can be five well-supported improvements and three carefully chosen briefs. The appropriate quantity is evidence-led, not a quota.

## 6. Produce a publishable brief, not a keyword dump
Use the supplied template. Include the intended task, main/supporting queries, evidence, existing destination, original contribution, factual material required, recommended structure, helpful questions, relevant links, media, conversion, owner and acceptance criteria. Prohibit unsupported claims and unnecessary name-dropping explicitly.

A FAQ candidate needs a specific question, source or hypothesis label, why the reader needs it, where it belongs, and what a good answer must establish. Do not add a vague FAQ solely to include every variant. Factual answers need verification before publication.

## 7. Verify and learn
Editorially review the answer, accuracy, intent fit and distinction from existing pages. After authorised release, monitor relevant exposure, visits and qualified conversions with scope/seasonality caveats. A first-pass plan remains a hypothesis about opportunity until real outcomes accumulate.
