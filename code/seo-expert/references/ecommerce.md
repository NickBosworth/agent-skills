# Ecommerce and catalogue SEO

## Model the catalogue before changing URLs
Identify categories, products, variants, filters, sort states, pagination, marketplace sellers and market/currency versions. Determine which routes represent independent products or valuable landing needs and which are user-interface states. Preserve identifiers, cart behaviour, inventory logic and existing integrations. [G36]

## Category and listing pages
Make important products reachable through a useful category hierarchy and ordinary links. Test empty states, pagination end states, out-of-stock filters and mobile interaction. Decide which faceted combinations merit indexable landing pages; demand alone is insufficient without inventory and a useful experience. Avoid unlimited parameter pages and blanket suppression of valuable categories. [G19, G25]

Give a category the information people need to choose, not a large irrelevant SEO paragraph before the products. Research terms can improve labels, guidance and filters without repeating every synonym.

## Product content
Capture accurate name, brand where relevant, model, compatibility, dimensions, materials, availability, price, shipping/returns and useful media appropriate to the product. Do not manufacture tests, reviews or performance claims. Add original information the business can actually substantiate rather than mechanically paraphrasing supplier text. Product attributes should help selection and resolve likely questions. [G03, G31]

## Variants and offers
Choose a coherent variant URL and canonical policy based on real differences and user needs; not every colour must become a separate SEO page. Review the current Google variant and product requirements, the actual catalogue model, and any Merchant Center feed. Keep visible data, structured data and feeds aligned, including currency, availability and price changes. [G31, G35]

## Availability lifecycle
Temporarily out-of-stock products can still help users when the page provides accurate status and useful next steps. Permanently discontinued products require a case-by-case choice: retain useful documentation, point to a genuinely relevant successor, or remove with an appropriate response. Do not redirect every discontinued item to a category/homepage regardless of relevance. Review business, support and backlink dependencies before changes. [G18, G23]

## Private and transactional states
Checkout, account, basket and internal operational routes are not automatically candidates for public indexing. Never weaken authentication or expose personal data to “fix” exclusion. Protect non-indexing rules appropriate to these journeys, while checking that they do not spill into public product templates.

## Measurement
Separate informational discovery from category/product visits and qualified transactions. Verify analytics implementation and transaction integrity; revenue changes need careful attribution and cohort/seasonality context. Prioritise shared template or catalogue-data fixes before manual one-off polishing across thousands of products.
