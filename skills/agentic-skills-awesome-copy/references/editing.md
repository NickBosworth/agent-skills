# Editing for substance, voice and movement

Use this guide for a substantial rewrite or a diagnosis of generic copy. The
patterns below are editorial observations, not an authorship classifier. Solve
the reader's problem; do not edit merely to remove every pattern.

## Recover the thought

Before polishing, say what the sentence means in ordinary language. If you cannot
find a useful meaning, decide whether it needs a supported fact or deletion.
Synonym substitution cannot rescue a sentence with nothing to say.

Build around an actor, action and relevant consequence where those are known.
An abstract phrase such as "workflow optimization capabilities" could refer to
several different things; naming the actual operation makes it useful. Do not
guess the operation just to make the wording more concrete.

Distinguish a feature from a benefit. "Exports CSV" describes a capability.
"Open the export in a spreadsheet" describes a use, if supported. "Halves your
reporting time" requires separate evidence. Stop the benefit chain before it
becomes speculation. Detail can be a constraint, process, object, setting or
tradeoff; it does not have to be a conveniently invented number.

For an article or essay, find the claim worth examining and the evidence that
makes it more than an opinion. An explicit point of view can improve a piece,
but do not manufacture certainty, outrage or a contrarian position. Write the
strongest version supported by the material.

## Diagnose the habit, then choose a repair

All fragments here are invented examples. None is a list of forbidden strings.

| Habit | What goes wrong | Repair | When the form earns its place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Generic opening: "In today's rapidly changing world…" | Delays the actual subject with context that fits anything | Start with the supplied development, observation or reader's task | A specific change in the world is the subject and is explained |
| Inflated promise: "Unlock unparalleled potential" | Claims importance without explaining an outcome | Name the actual use or supported result | A large claim has suitable evidence and scope |
| Stock vocabulary: "elevate", "seamless", "robust", "delve" | A cluster can substitute mood for meaning | Choose the precise action; do not replace these mechanically | The word is literal, technically apt or characteristic of the voice |
| Noun pile: "implementation of the improvement process" | Hides who does what behind abstractions | Use the relevant verb and actor | A precise noun is a recognized term or the focus of the sentence |
| Decorative tail: "…, fostering success and ensuring excellence" | Adds a benefit that is vague, unsupported or already stated | Explain the actual causal step, give it a real sentence, or cut it | The consequence follows from the evidence and adds needed meaning |
| Automatic contrast: "It's not just X. It's Y." | Performs profundity without clarifying a distinction | State the useful claim directly or name the actual difference | The reader might reasonably confuse two things with different consequences |
| Repeated triads: "simple, smart and powerful" | Produces a slogan cadence without information | Keep the one relevant quality or give the distinct facts | Three items have separate jobs or a deliberate rhetorical purpose |
| Forced fragments: "Less friction. More freedom. Total control." | Manufactures drama and monotonous beats | Reconnect the thought and place emphasis once | A short fragment provides appropriate emphasis in that genre |
| Formulaic coverage: "Whether you're a beginner or an expert…" | Tries to address everyone and says little to anyone | Write for the actual reader; distinguish needs when they differ | The two cases genuinely need different guidance |
| Canned empathy: "We understand how frustrating this must be" | Claims feelings instead of helping | State what happened, acknowledge the consequence, give a supported next step | A sincere acknowledgment is useful and proportionate to the relationship |
| Mechanical scaffolding: identical sections, constant bold, mandatory recap | Makes the organization more prominent than the thought | Group by reader need and remove duplicate summaries | Instructions, comparison or a long reference benefit from explicit structure |
| Generic ending: "The future is bright. The possibilities are endless." | Evades the consequence of the piece | End on the useful implication, image, decision or next step | An expansive ending is earned by the preceding argument or artistic brief |

Punctuation does not make weak copy human. Retain dashes, colons, parentheses,
questions and semicolons when they clarify emphasis or relationships. Respect
an explicit house-style restriction without presenting it as a universal rule.

## Keep the speaker recognizable

Use a supplied voice sample as evidence of choices, not a bag of catchphrases.
Notice how the speaker names things, makes a point, handles uncertainty, jokes,
and moves between short and long sentences. Translate broad adjectives into
observable behavior: "confident" may mean direct claims with their limits stated;
"warm" may mean considerate explanations rather than extra exclamation marks.

Distinguish voice from situational tone. A playful product can speak plainly about
a failed payment. An expert can explain something simply without pretending to be
a novice. An approachable message can remain formal. Do not treat contractions,
first names, slang, swearing, typos or casual asides as ingredients required for
human writing. Respect the reader's emotions without asserting you know them.

Keep unusual phrasing that carries the author's observation or character. Remove
awkwardness that blocks comprehension. If a line is already strong, keep it.
Change the smallest amount that improves the assigned outcome; avoid imposing
your own voice merely to make the edit visible.

## Give sentences somewhere to go

- Put established information near the beginning and the useful development where
  it can receive emphasis. Avoid carrying an important fact in an afterthought.
- Favor verbs over abstractions when the action matters. Use passive voice when
  the actor is unknown, irrelevant or would distract from the affected person.
- Let a paragraph advance one part of the idea; connect the next paragraph through
  the actual relationship. "Moreover" and "Furthermore" cannot supply a missing
  connection, but a necessary transition should not be cut for a punchier look.
- Vary sentence openings and lengths when repetition becomes accidental. Repetition
  can also establish a motif, parallel instruction or emphasis. Preserve it when
  it works; keep terms consistent instead of chasing lexical variety.
- Save the shortest sentence for a point that deserves weight. Do not turn every
  clause into a separate punch line or every paragraph into a one-line post.
- Read through at a normal speaking pace to catch breathless clauses, unintended
  ambiguity and emphasis in the wrong place. Written genres have their own rhythms;
  a legal explanation need not sound like a voice message.

There is no required sentence-length distribution, readability grade or ratio of
rare words. Measurements can expose a constraint or help a specific accessibility
task; they cannot substitute for judgment about meaning and audience.

## Add life without adding fiction

Use a supplied detail, a clear comparison, a fresh but understandable verb, or a
restrained image that belongs to the subject. A metaphor should illuminate the
idea; check its internal logic and avoid mixing several images in one paragraph.
Plain language can carry emotion through selection and emphasis. A lyrical brief
can justify more texture than a form label.

An anecdote needs an actual source or an explicitly fictional context. Do not
invent memories, customers, dialogue or firsthand use as a shortcut to intimacy.
Keep real quotes accurate and attributed as required. Draw lessons from reference
copy without cloning its distinctive slogans or sentences.

## Review according to the work mode

For a rewrite, check the result against the original facts and the brief; do not
assume every deleted qualification was filler. For a critique, explain a concrete
problem and its reader cost, without declaring a probability of AI authorship.
For alternatives, judge whether each offers a different idea or emphasis and
whether the body can fulfill its promise. A restrained original can beat all the
new candidates. Stop when the piece serves its purpose and satisfies its constraints.
