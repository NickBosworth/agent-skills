# Titles and formats

Load the section for the actual deliverable. These are selection criteria, not
templates to paste into every brief. The user's established format takes precedence.

## Titles, headlines and subject lines

Decide what the title must do. A help-page title helps someone find an answer; an
essay title sets up an idea; a product headline introduces an offer. Cleverness
that obscures a form's purpose is a different tradeoff from an image in a magazine
headline. Read the underlying piece before promising its contents.

Choose an angle from the supplied material:

| Angle | Use when | Question to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task or answer | The reader needs to do or understand something | What will this help them do? |
| Concrete use or benefit | The product solves a known problem | What becomes possible, and through what actual capability? |
| Observation or point of view | The piece has a supported insight | What is the useful thing the writer noticed? |
| Tension or tradeoff | Two goals or facts genuinely pull against each other | What specific conflict is worth exploring? |
| Detail or image | A supplied detail carries the story or mood | What makes this subject memorable without hiding it? |

Generate distinct angles when alternatives are useful, then tighten the wording.
Do not produce several versions of the same title with different adjectives.
Follow the requested number; if it is unspecified and exploration is appropriate,
a few strong options are enough. Recommend a fit rather than declaring an
unobserved winner.

Check each candidate:

- It identifies the subject or offers a relevant promise; it works outside the
  surrounding page when it will appear in search, an inbox or a list.
- The piece fulfills that promise. A number matches the actual contents, a
  question is answered, and a claim keeps its conditions and evidential strength.
- Its most useful words appear early enough for scanning. Avoid throat-clearing
  labels such as "An introduction to" when they add nothing to the brief.
- Its phrasing belongs to the speaker. Do not force colons, "ultimate guide",
  "the secret to", rhetorical questions or a dramatic reversal on every topic.
- Curiosity comes from a useful detail or unresolved idea, not a withheld basic
  fact, fake surprise, invented exclusivity or a promise stronger than the body.
- It meets the supplied length, capitalization and required-term constraints.

For a subject line, let preview text add information instead of repeating it.
Never add "Re:" or a false deadline to imply an exchange or urgency that did not
happen. For SEO titles, keep the search term where it is natural and accurate;
distinguish the search title from the visible headline if the task calls for both.
There is no universal character count that guarantees search display or inbox fit.
Check an explicit limit exactly, and rendered truncation only if the surface is
available. Do not promise higher clicks or conversions from an editorial judgment.

## Website and commercial copy

Start with what the offer does for the intended reader. Use the subheading to
explain the capability or detail the headline cannot hold. Let a feature section
add evidence or address a different reader need instead of echoing the hero.

Persuasion comes from relevance, credible detail and a clear next step. Explain
price, eligibility, commitments or limitations where the reader needs them.
Do not manufacture social proof, comparisons, scarcity, certainty, speed or
financial outcomes. Preserve a valid approved claim; flag an unsupported one
instead of laundering it into more fluent prose.

Match the CTA to its destination: viewing an example, starting a paid order and
requesting a conversation are different actions. Use the exact product term
where consistency matters. A short evocative line can work when adjacent copy
supplies the explanation; a whole page of unexplained slogans cannot.

## Articles, essays and longer prose

Find the real argument, story or observation. Open with its most useful entry
point rather than a generic overview of the topic's importance. Develop the
evidence and consequences in a sequence suited to the piece. A narrative can
withhold a resolution when that is part of its form; a troubleshooting answer
usually benefits from giving the answer early.

Give each paragraph a reason to follow the last. Vary the scale between claim,
detail and implication instead of repeating a thesis after every example. Keep
nuance, necessary context and purposeful digressions. Avoid turning a considered
essay into a stack of slogans in the name of brevity.

Use headings when they help navigation; make them say something about their
sections. End on the consequence, decision or image the piece has earned.
Include a summary when the genre or reader need calls for one, not automatically.

## Emails, newsletters and social posts

Respect the relationship. A service notice, an invitation and a note to a longtime
colleague need different openings and levels of explanation. Generic pleasantries
can be removed when they delay the purpose; a real greeting or personal detail
can be appropriate. Never invent a relationship, anecdote or firsthand experience.

Make the reason for writing and the useful information easy to find. Keep an ask
clear and proportionate. Give a date, location or next step when supplied and
relevant; do not fabricate them for completeness.

For newsletters, build around an observation or useful item rather than a string
of promotional announcements. For social posts, write to the platform and audience
without automatically using a suspense hook, one-sentence paragraphs, emojis,
hashtags or engagement bait. Use these only when the brief and voice warrant them.
Drafting copy alone does not authorize posting or messaging anyone.

## Product text, buttons, errors and empty states

Clarity and truthful state take priority over personality. A label names the action
or object. Help text explains the missing context at the moment it is needed.
An error states what went wrong and the available recovery; do not invent a cause,
saved data, a successful payment or a safe retry. A pending state is not success.
An empty state explains what belongs there and the supported next action.

Use link and button wording that still makes sense in its accessibility context.
Avoid relying on a control's color or screen position. Preserve glossary terms,
translation keys, placeholders, markup and variable syntax. If changing a live
interface's strings, inspect where they are used; one label may serve several states.
Do not change behavior or add help text to compensate for an unrelated design flaw
without authority for that work.

## Formal, technical and international writing

Human writing can be measured, expert or ceremonial. Preserve accurate terminology
and required wording, including negation, exceptions and uncertainty. Clarify
surrounding prose without implying a legal, medical or financial claim was reviewed
by an expert when it was not. Do not compress away a critical instruction or condition.

For multilingual or international audiences, keep the requested language and locale.
Use clear syntax, consistent terms and culturally appropriate formality. Avoid
importing English idioms, jokes, contractions or sentence rules into another
language. Repetition may help translation or accessibility; arbitrary synonym
variety can make both worse. If native-language nuance or rendered fit cannot be
checked, state that specific limitation when it matters.
